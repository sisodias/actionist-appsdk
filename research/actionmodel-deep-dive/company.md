# Company and business-model readout

## Identity and positioning

Action Model’s public story is “community-owned AI” built around a Large Action Model (LAM). The distinction from a normal language model is operational: a LAM is intended to observe a screen, decide through an action policy/tree, click/type/scroll/navigate, and verify the outcome. The company frames GUI interaction data as the scarce asset that will determine who controls the next generation of AI automation.

Primary public identity sources:

- [Action Model home](https://actionmodel.com/)
- [About page](https://actionmodel.com/about)
- [What is the Action Model?](https://docs.actionmodel.com/)
- [Large Action Models vs Large Language Models](https://actionmodel.com/blog/large-action-models-vs-large-language-models)

The brand is deliberately political/economic as well as technical. The blog and social feed repeatedly use “AI will replace jobs,” “Big Tech owns the future,” “take back control,” and “own the future” frames. This creates a strong community identity, but it also means public messaging is often a thesis or rallying cry rather than a measured product claim.

## Product family

| Product | Public job-to-be-done | Monetization / strategic role |
|---|---|---|
| Action Model training extension | Capture opted-in browser interactions and reward contributors | Proprietary action-data acquisition and community growth |
| ActionFi | Let partners reward verified human product usage or tasks | Partner campaigns, token/reward economics, distribution |
| Actionist | Execute computer-use agents and workflows across apps | SaaS seats, storage, reactions, cloud browser, marketplace |
| Workflow / agent marketplace | Publish, discover, buy, subscribe to, or sell workflows and agents | Creator revenue share and token consumption |
| LAM / Action Tree | Underlying model/policy trained on screens, actions, context and sequences | Strategic moat and future model utility |
| $LAM economy | Fuel actions, reward training, distribute creator/community value | Token utility, burn, ecosystem incentives; TGE/contract details remain open |

This is best understood as a flywheel: more contributors create more training data; better action capability increases demand for automation; ActionFi gives partners a distribution and verification channel; rewards and referrals bring more contributors; the marketplace creates a supply of automations.

## Company-reported scale claims

The following are public claims, not independently reconciled facts:

- The main site says “trusted by 500,000 people” / “500,000+ people.”
- The Chrome Store listing shows 80,000 users and 398 ratings; Chrome Stats showed 90,000 in an August 2026 snapshot.
- LinkedIn launch retrospective posts claim milestones such as 250K+, 300K, 350K and 500K community/users, 100K extension downloads, 500M training points and 20M hours. The changing figures likely reflect successive campaign snapshots, but the underlying definitions are not provided.
- The ActionFi partner page shows dashboard-style numbers such as 36K users, 30K rewarded tokens and 256,845 `$LAM`, plus a Binance example. These appear to be product/marketing examples, not an audited KPI report.
- Official docs show “10,000+ workflows,” top creator earnings above $47K/month, 10–20 hours/week saved, and example ROI calculations. These are useful hypotheses about intended value, not validated benchmarks.

The key diligence requirement is a metric dictionary: define registered account, active user, extension install, training contributor, verified ActionFi user, paying Actionist seat, workflow execution, and token holder, then request a time series for each.

## Distribution and partnerships

The public partner graph includes:

- **SIXR Cricket:** announced in current ActionFi launch messaging as the first official partner, with a reported $100K reward pool.
- **TaskOn:** a campaign/distribution partner associated with a public case-study claim of 8,991 verified participants; direct case-study evidence should be requested.
- **Ethos Network:** official partnership announcement for reputation-based early access.
- **ActionFi platform partners:** the SDK docs and partner page target SaaS, exchanges, ecosystems, launchpads, KOLs, agencies, and any site/desktop GUI where a partner wants verified usage.

The partner value proposition is unusually specific: reward an action that can be observed and verified in a real browser session, rather than paying for a click, view, or API event. The counter-risk is that “verified” must be defined technically: what is captured, what proves a real user, how replay/bot/farm behavior is excluded, and how disputes are resolved.

## Content and growth model

The company’s public acquisition loop is visible across docs, social posts, and referral rules:

1. Lead with urgency: AI displacement and Big Tech ownership.
2. Offer agency: contribute data, train the model, earn points/rewards, “own” the future.
3. Add action-based incentives: extension training, ActionFi tasks, quests, referrals, ambassador programs.
4. Use product education: Actionist demos, no-code workflows, marketplace and LAM explainers.
5. Convert partners with real-usage campaigns and a shared reward pool.

This is a powerful crypto-community growth system. It also raises three client-facing questions: how much engagement is product-led versus reward-led, whether contributors understand data use, and whether the reward economy can remain attractive after token launch/utility changes.

## Corporate and diligence gaps

Before treating the company as an enterprise-ready client, obtain:

- exact contracting entity, jurisdiction, beneficial ownership and signing authority;
- funding, treasury, token allocation, contract address, chain, audit and TGE status;
- privacy/DPA/subprocessor list, retention/deletion mechanics and cross-border transfer basis;
- extension and Actionist security audits, incident history, responsible-disclosure process and SBOM;
- KPI definitions and raw exports behind user, points, rewards, training-hour and partner-campaign claims;
- partner references and a redacted ActionFi campaign report showing fraud controls and payout reconciliation.
