## Executive outcome

The first pass had 31 company or company-group rows. This expansion adds **36 distinct product surfaces**, producing a **67-row combined product-surface census**. The target of 50+ total surfaces is therefore met without repeating a baseline product row.

The expansion changes the market picture in four ways:

1. The “prompt-to-app” category is broader than the first-pass benchmark set. Blink, Softgen, Databutton, Anything/Create, WeWeb, GlideOS, AppSheet, and Figma Make cover different combinations of mobile, data-native, exportable, visual, and design-led generation.
2. Enterprise low-code platforms are a major adjacent market rather than a footnote. Power Apps, Salesforce Platform/Agentforce, ServiceNow App Engine, Mendix, Superblocks, Creatio, Workato, and Automation Anywhere expose stronger tenancy, deployment, governance, and procurement boundaries than most consumer builders, but usually at the cost of vendor-shaped data/runtime contracts.
3. The coding-agent market is converging on asynchronous execution, isolated environments, PR/branch handoff, and usage metering. Devin Desktop (formerly Windsurf), Devin Cloud, Augment Code, Jules, and Amazon Q add distinct agent/runtime shapes beyond the baseline Cursor, Claude Code, and GitHub Copilot rows.
4. Browser and execution infrastructure is becoming a metered substrate. Browser Use, MultiOn, UiPath, Apify, Cloudflare Browser Run, CodeSandbox, Codeanywhere, and Ona/Gitpod expand the operation and execution planes. None is treated as proof of Actionist authority, tenant isolation, evidence, or rollback.

The strongest new evidence is not that one vendor wins. It is that commercial products split the problem into different contracts: visual/code generation, data and permissions, agent execution, browser control, workflow actions, or isolated compute. A portable Actionist contract would need to compose those boundaries while retaining provenance, authority, verification, recovery, and economics. That is an `I` (inference), not an implemented capability.

## Baseline and expansion boundary

The immutable first-pass baseline is [`company-landscape.md`](./company-landscape.md). It contains 31 rows and must not be silently rewritten as part of this phase. This packet adds only product surfaces not represented as baseline rows.

Two overlap rules apply:

- **Distinct product surface:** a new product from a company already represented in the baseline can be added when it supplies a materially different builder, agent, workflow, browser, design, or execution contract. Google AppSheet and Jules are distinct from the baseline Google AI Studio/Firebase Studio/Antigravity row. Microsoft Power Apps is distinct from the baseline GitHub Copilot cloud-agent row.
- **No fake company multiplication:** the combined count is reported as product surfaces first and organization-family count second. A parent company appearing in both phases is not claimed to be a new company merely because a new product was inspected.

## Evidence vocabulary and row contract

- `E` — directly inspected first-party product, documentation, pricing, API, or repository artifact.
- `D` — documented first-party claim, not independently authenticated by a live run.
- `I` — inference from multiple evidence points; never used as a product-capability fact.
- `U` — unknown, not found, gated, private, contract-only, or not established in the reviewed source set.

Each expansion row records the observed date in the evidence cell, a capability taxonomy, maturity, pricing or positioning where visible, API/import/export/deployment/white-label/marketplace boundaries where observable, separate authority/recovery/tenancy/portability notes, a disposition, an open question, and direct first-party URLs. “Candidate,” “reference,” and “hold” are research dispositions; none is an admission decision.

Tags:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `P2A` | prompt-to-app or full-stack builder |
| `INT` | internal-tool, business-app, or data-native builder |
| `DTC` | design-to-code, visual editing, or website builder |
| `AGT` | coding agent or agent runtime |
| `CUSE` | browser, RPA, or computer-use operation |
| `WF` | workflow, integration, or orchestration platform |
| `EXE` | sandbox, cloud IDE, or execution substrate |
| `GOV` | identity, approval, audit, tenancy, security, or release controls |
| `REG` | registry, template, component, connector, or marketplace surface |

## Adversarial reconciliation

### What the first-party pages directly establish

- WeWeb explicitly documents Vue.js export and self-hosting; this is stronger portability evidence than a generic “download code” claim, but the page does not prove backend/auth/schema export or migration completeness.
- Figma documents prompt-to-functional-prototype, editable code, Supabase support, Make kits, MCP, templates, and publishing; Figma’s own organization help page also limits the current backend behavior and notes mock-code cases. Positive and negative evidence are retained together.
- Enterprise low-code vendors expose materially stronger controls: Superblocks lists self-hosting, SSO/SCIM, RBAC, audit, environments, testing, and observability; Mendix lists private/public deployment and database tenancy; ServiceNow and Salesforce expose contract/edition boundaries; UiPath exposes own-premise, credential vault, HITL, and CI/CD options.
- Usage meters are now part of product contracts: Blink, Make, Pipedream, Lindy, Workato, Devin, Augment, Amazon Q, Browser Use, CodeSandbox, Cloudflare, Glide, and AppSheet all expose credits, tasks, compute, users, or usage gates in first-party sources.
- Lifecycle failure is evidence: Relay’s shutdown notice explicitly instructs customers to export and distinguishes continued workflow operation from continuity after the shutdown date.

### What remains inference or unknown

- **“50+ products means market coverage.”** `I/U`: 67 surfaces are substantial bounded coverage, not an exhaustive census. Regional, private, behind-login, stealth, procurement-only, and unindexed products remain unknown.
- **“Export means portability.”** `I/U`: only WeWeb’s frontend export claim is relatively explicit; all products need a schema/auth/secret/asset/dependency/deployment check before portability is promoted.
- **“Enterprise governance means safe side effects.”** `I/U`: permissions, audit logs, SSO, and approvals are fragments; they do not prove idempotency, postconditions, recovery, or cross-system rollback.
- **“AI agent pricing is comparable.”** `U`: credits, tokens, visits, ACUs, OCUs, browser hours, workflow compute, LOC, and seats measure different units and cannot be ranked without a common workload.
- **“Open-core means reusable.”** `U`: source availability, product license, hosted terms, plugins, dependencies, generated assets, and client redistribution are separate questions.
- **“Marketplace means vetted block registry.”** `U`: AppExchange, Glide/Make/Pipedream/Apify ecosystems, Figma Community, and templates expose distribution surfaces, not Actionist provenance or admission receipts.

## Findings for the Action Model thesis

1. **The portability wedge is now more testable.** WeWeb and Blink make explicit self-host/code-ownership claims, while Figma Make and vendor-native platforms expose narrower or conditional backend behavior. A future benchmark should compare complete handoff artifacts, not UI output.
2. **Enterprise governance is abundant but vendor-shaped.** Superblocks, Power Apps, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Mendix, UiPath, Workato, and Automation Anywhere show that buyers value identity, audit, environment, secrets, deployment, and support controls. The unresolved question is whether those contracts can be composed across a client-owned data boundary.
3. **Usage units are a product design problem.** Credits, tasks, visits, ACUs, OCUs, browser hours, compute seconds, LOC, and per-conversation billing are not interchangeable. Actionist needs a successful-outcome meter with explicit budget, stop, and evidence semantics before comparing vendors.
4. **Workflow approvals and browser operation remain separate planes.** Lindy, Relay, Workato, UiPath, Browser Use, MultiOn, Apify, and Cloudflare each cover slices. None was promoted to a complete operation contract with authority, idempotency, verification, recovery, and evidence.
5. **Lifecycle is part of portability.** Relay’s shutdown demonstrates that export and continuity are separate requirements. Ona’s and Windsurf’s rebrands demonstrate that product identity and documentation can change quickly. A client delivery packet must not depend on a vendor’s continued existence without an exit path.
