The idea in one paragraph

A generator starts from zero on every build: its thousandth app is produced against the same blank context as its first. A library compounds — every build ranks the shelf for the next one. So the asset we are building is the library, and any single app is a demonstration of it. That is also what makes cheap models viable: they are not asked to invent an app, only to select, adapt and glue parts that already work.

Why this approach wins

  • It compounds. Every conversion and every qualification improves the next selection. A competitor can copy a corpus; they cannot copy an evidence graph they never accumulated.
  • Cheap models stay in scope. Bounded edits against a known-good scaffold are a different problem from free-generating an app — which is what makes a MiniMax-class budget realistic rather than aspirational.
  • We own the code. The default shape is to fork the donor and integrate it natively, so the code can actually be changed. Anything mounted intact is recorded as debt with a named path to absorption.
  • Licence is read, not assumed. 24 frameworks govern selection and rating. Two examples from the work: a widely-used search dependency whose permissive badge hides an ELv2-only carve-out in the exact subdirectory containing the feature we would need; and a repo whose licence forbids removing its branding — a legal problem wearing an engineering costume. Both were caught by reading the licence body rather than the badge.

The funnel is steep on purpose. Most registered sources never become blocks, and that is the point of having a shelf with a gate on it rather than a pile of repositories. Notably, stars, tags, age and README claims are explicitly not treated as evidence — a source with no runtime evidence cannot be rated at all.
